Trade-finance document fraud detection · prototype

Catch fraud in the gaps between documents.

VeriDoc reads a company's whole loan application (bank statements, invoices, orders, shipping papers, tax forms and accounts), checks every document against the others and against official records, and tells an analyst exactly what looks wrong and where. A team of AI agents looks for what the fixed rules cannot, but code checks every claim they make, and a person always makes the decision.

Built for Green Link Digital Bank (GLDB). Synthetic data only; not for production use.

The problem

Each document looks fine. The fraud is in how they disagree.

A bank lends against trade: the company has sold goods, or is buying them, and needs money until it is paid. To prove the trade is real, it sends a pack of documents. Every genuine trade leaves the same trail, and every link in that trail can be faked.

1 · Buyer

Purchase order

The buyer asks for goods at an agreed price.
2 · Carrier

Bill of lading / delivery order

Proof the goods actually moved.
3 · Seller

Invoice

The bill for what was ordered and shipped.
4 · Bank

Payment on the statement

The buyer's money arriving to settle the invoice.
Order never issuedThe invoice names an order that does not exist.
Goods not shippedThe invoice bills more than was carried.
OverbilledThe invoice charges more than the order and the payment.
Wrong payerThe sale is paid by a company with no part in it.

Around the trade, more can be wrong

The statement

Edited bank figures

A credit is raised and the PDF saved again, so revenue looks bigger than it was.

The company

Who it says it is

Forms disagree with the official register on a director or address; the firm is struck off; a shareholder is sanctioned.

The money

Circular flows

Money paid to a supplier comes back as a "customer receipt"; a supplier is secretly run by the applicant's own director.

A human analyst catches these by reading everything and cross-checking it by hand, which is slow, and easy to get wrong on the fiftieth application of the week. VeriDoc does the cross-checking and shows its working, so the analyst can spend their time on judgement.

How it works

Read, check, decide, explain, record.

An application goes through twelve stages in five steps. The fixed rules alone set the risk score and the route. The AI team runs alongside them and can only add verified leads for the analyst; it can never move the score.

The twelve stages

Each stage has its own page in the app, beside the document viewer, and the arrow keys step through them. Open any stage to see what it does.

Rules & score

Fixed rules decide the risk. No AI touches the score.

Every rule is a plain, deterministic test: the same documents always give the same findings. Each finding records what was expected, what was observed, and the exact quote and page it came from, so the analyst can click it and see the line boxed on the document.

Rule groupChecksWhat it looks for
Document integrity6Do the bank balances add up row by row? Are dates, totals and periods consistent inside each document?
Forensics5Was the PDF edited and saved again? Is there hidden or white text? Do fonts change mid-line? Does what the page shows differ from what the file stores?
Cross-document13Does each invoice match its order, shipment and payment? Who paid? Do names and identities agree across forms?
Registry & screening6Is the company in ACRA and live? Do the accounts match what was filed? Are any names on the UN sanctions lists or in FATF high-risk countries?
Timeline2Is anything dated before its order, or before the company existed?
Compliance & completeness16Are the required documents here? Are the FATCA/CRS tax forms filled in correctly?

From findings to a score

Only fraud findings score. Each maps to a named signal worth fixed points (a medium finding is 15), and the points add up to a score out of 100. Missing documents never add risk; they make the application incomplete instead, because "unfinished" and "suspicious" are different problems.

From a score to a route

Fast track

Low score, complete application. A light check; 5% are still sampled for quality assurance.

Standard review

Medium score, or incomplete, or the documents were hard to read. An analyst works the case.

Enhanced review

High score or a hard flag. Enhanced due diligence, and the MLRO must sign off before approval.

Hard flags force enhanced review whatever the score: a sanctions match, a company that is not in the register, hidden text aimed at automated review, or a prompt attack hidden in a document. The rule is "most cautious wins": a signal can raise the route but never lower it.

The AI team

Looking where rules cannot, under strict supervision.

Some fraud is a pattern, not a broken rule: money that goes out and comes back, a supplier that shares a director with the applicant. A team of AI agents on Amazon Bedrock looks for these, using read-only tools over the case. They propose; code checks; people decide.

Supervisor Claude Sonnet 4.6

Reads the rule findings, writes a brief for each specialist, then reconciles their verified leads into a case memo.

Four safeguards

Read-onlyAgents can search and read the case. They cannot change a document, a finding or a route.
IsolatedEach specialist sees only its own brief and tool results; a lead citing a result it never received is refused.
Verified by codeEvery lead is tested against the documents (verified, contradicted or unchecked) before an analyst sees it.
Log-onlyA shadow scorer may propose a review. It is recorded beside the real route and never replaces it.

Leads that use verdict words ("this is fraud") or cite nothing are withheld, and still shown in the trace so nothing is hidden. Text in a document that tries to instruct the AI is caught by a Bedrock guardrail before any model reads it.

People stay in charge

The system routes and explains. People decide.

Analyst

Works the case

Reads each finding on its page, follows the task queue, can override a check with a reason, and drafts a request for information to the applicant.

MLRO

Signs off enhanced reviews

An enhanced case cannot be approved until a different person, the Money Laundering Reporting Officer, records a decision. Declining is never held back.

Auditor

Can replay any decision

Every stage and action goes to an append-only log with file fingerprints and rule and policy versions, so a past route can be reproduced exactly.

How well it works

Measured honestly, misses included.

Testing on our own demo packs only proves the system catches what we planted. So we also ran a blind test: a separate AI model wrote 60 applicants from public fraud typologies, the system was frozen first, and it was scored against an answer key it never saw.

Why the misses happened

  • 5 orders never issued: a rule found every one, but a single medium finding scores 15, just under the review threshold of 20. The AI team's shadow scorer proposed review for all five.
  • 5 round trips: no rule looks for this by design; it is the AI team's job. It found and code-verified 4; one (H-041) still went through and is not yet diagnosed.
  • 2 false alarms: the test's document generator reused the same two officer names across many companies, so the register really did show a shared, undeclared director. Code verified it correctly; it just was not planted.

All data is synthetic and laid out in formats the parser was written for. These numbers show the design works on unseen cases; they are not a detection rate on real bank applications.

Limits

What this prototype is not, yet.

Known limits

  • Synthetic data only; real documents come in layouts the parser has not seen, and photos or screenshots of documents are not handled.
  • The Claude models use a global inference profile, so requests may be processed outside Singapore: fine for demo data, not for real applicants.
  • Analysts cannot yet record a decision on an AI lead itself.
  • One shared sign-in, local files for storage and an in-memory job tracker: not multi-user, not tamper-proof.

Each of these has a place in the road to production below.

Road to production

From prototype to a bank's live system.

Five phases, in the order they depend on each other. Each ends at a gate the bank can check before the next one starts. Durations depend on the bank's own processes, so none are given.

0

Phase 0 · now

Finish the prototype

Fix what the blind test and our own reviews exposed.

  • Re-run the AI team's evaluation (about US$3), since the saved one predates the November rule changes; find out why it missed H-041.
  • Let a lone "order never issued" finding reach review: today it scores 15, just under the threshold of 20.
  • Stop the test generator reusing officer names, the cause of both false alarms, then run a fresh blind test.
  • Let analysts record a decision on each AI lead, not only on rule findings.
  • Produce the risk report in the shape the sponsor asked for: key findings, risks with evidence, follow-up actions, severity from Low to Critical.

Done whena new blind test is run and every miss is fixed or explained.

1

Phase 1

A production-grade platform

Replace the one-server setup with something a bank's technology risk team can approve.

  • Data stays in Singapore: enable Claude Sonnet 5 in ap-southeast-1, with a small code change (newer models reject the temperature and forced tool choice we send).
  • A login per person: Cognito or the bank's single sign-on, with analyst, MLRO and admin roles, replacing the shared password and typed-in names.
  • Storage that holds up: encrypted S3 for uploads, DynamoDB for cases, and S3 Object Lock so the audit log cannot be altered.
  • Reliable processing: SQS or Step Functions in place of the in-memory job tracker, which loses progress on a restart.
  • Private network: no public tunnel; the app sits inside the bank's network or behind a load balancer with a web application firewall.
  • Operations: monitoring and alerts, backups, separate test and production environments, and a faster test run.

Why banks do this MAS's technology risk and outsourcing guidelines expect access control, a tamper-evident audit trail and a known data location before customer data touches a system.

Done whena security review and penetration test pass with no high findings.

2

Phase 2

Real documents and real data sources

Handle what actually arrives: the sponsor says PDFs, scans, screenshots and photos.

  • Read scans and photos with Textract AnalyzeDocument and AnalyzeExpense (tested on 28 October, not yet built).
  • Add image forensics: re-saving traces, copied-and-pasted areas, pasted signatures and stamps.
  • Replace the mock ACRA and IRAS service with the real ACRA API and Myinfo Business.
  • Add a commercial screening provider for sanctions, politically exposed persons and adverse news, beside the MAS and UN lists.
  • Test the parser on anonymised historical applications from the bank and on a hand-tampered set.

Done whenextraction accuracy and tamper detection are measured on real documents, not synthetic ones.

3

Phase 3

Validation and governance

Earn approval from the bank's model risk and compliance teams.

  • Independent validation: someone outside the build team re-tests the system against a pre-registered plan, as the blind test did.
  • Thresholds the bank owns: the sponsor says none are fixed in practice, so ours stay in configuration and the bank signs off its own.
  • Data protection and fair use of AI: a PDPA impact assessment and a review against MAS's FEAT principles (fairness, ethics, accountability, transparency).
  • Records: a model inventory entry and change control; rule and policy versions already let any past decision be replayed.

Why banks do this Any model that affects a decision is validated independently and has documented limits before it goes live, the same way credit models are.

Done whenmodel risk, compliance and the MLRO sign off the system and its limits.

4

Phase 4

Pilot, then rollout

Prove it on live applications without putting them at risk.

  • Shadow mode: VeriDoc runs on live applications while analysts decide exactly as today; its routes are compared with theirs.
  • Assist mode: analysts see its findings as they work; measure time saved, frauds caught, false alarms and how often each check is overridden.
  • Go-live gates agreed in advance: the catch rate, false-alarm rate and time saved that must be met.
  • After launch: watch for drift, feed override statistics back into the rules, and keep sanctions and FATF lists updated on a schedule.

Why banks do this A new model runs as a challenger beside the current process and is promoted only on evidence, the pattern VeriDoc's shadow scorer already follows.

Done whenthe pilot meets the agreed gates and the bank approves live use.

What does not change on the way: the rules set the score and the route, the AI only proposes and code verifies it, and a person decides, with MLRO sign-off for enhanced reviews.

Glossary

Terms used above.

Trade finance
Loans that fund a company's buying or selling of goods until it is paid.
ACRA
Singapore's company register: directors, shareholders, address, status.
IRAS / NOA
Singapore's tax authority; a Notice of Assessment shows a person's assessed income.
UEN
A Singapore company's registration number.
FATCA / CRS
Tax-residence forms banks must collect for international tax reporting.
Bill of lading
The carrier's receipt for goods shipped by sea; a delivery order does the same locally.
KYC
Know your customer: checking who a company and its people really are.
MLRO
Money Laundering Reporting Officer, who owns the bank's anti-money-laundering decisions.
EDD
Enhanced due diligence: the deeper review behind the enhanced route.
FATF
The global anti-money-laundering body; it lists high-risk countries.
Round trip
Money sent out that comes back disguised as new income.
Related party
A counterparty linked to the applicant, such as through a shared director.
Hard flag
A finding serious enough to force enhanced review on its own.
Lead
An AI agent's proposed pattern, with citations, waiting to be verified by code.
